I have a physics background and grew up in an

engineering-centric household. I’m still more an

我父亲是一位极具天赋的电气与机械工程师。我小时候，我们一起制作模型飞机和电路板。他辅导我，而我当时甚至浑然不觉。155我还做过模型火箭之类的东西。在南非，没有现成的火箭。我得去药店买火箭燃料的原料，自己混合，然后装进管子里。156

展开英文原文

My dad is an extremely talented electrical and mechanical engineer. We built model airplanes and circuit boards together when I was a kid. He tutored me, and I didn’t even know it at the time.155 I also did things like make model rockets. In South Africa, there were no premade rockets. I had to go to the chemist to get the ingredients for rocket fuel, mix it, and put it in a pipe.156

我身边到处都是“工程味”十足的东西。每当我问起，总能得到事物如何运作的真实解释。157我小时候非常怕黑。但后来我明白，“黑暗”只是可见光波段——四百到七百纳米——缺少光子。我想，害怕缺少光子实在可笑。从那以后，我就不再怕黑了。158

展开英文原文

There were lots of “engineery” things around me. When I asked for an explanation, I got the true explanation of how things work.157 When I was a little kid, I was really scared of the dark. But I came to understand “dark” just means the absence of photons in the visible wavelength—four hundred to seven hundred nanometers. I thought, well it’s silly to be afraid of a lack of photons. Then I wasn’t afraid of the dark anymore.158

在南非长大的我，从小就对技术非常着迷。159我们的第一台电脑附带了自学编程的书籍。那是我见过最酷的东西。大约十二岁时，我开始编程，并出售自己做的游戏来买新游戏。我完全着了迷。我会花钱买更好的电脑、《龙与地下城》模组之类的东西。我就是个超级书呆子。160

展开英文原文

I was very technology oriented as a kid growing up in South Africa.159 With our first computer came books to teach yourself programming. This was the coolest thing I’d ever seen. When I was about twelve, I started programming and selling my own games to buy new games. I was hooked. I’d spend money on better computers, Dungeons & Dragons modules, and things like that. I was nerdmaster3000.160

我并不算特别孤僻，但相当书呆子气。我总是在阅读。读书、玩电脑、看漫画，诸如此类。161

展开英文原文

I wasn’t that much of a loner, but I was quite bookish. I was reading all the time. I would be reading, working on my computer, reading comics, that kind of thing.161

年轻时，我不确定长大后要做什么。我觉得发明东西的想法会很酷，因为我读到过阿瑟·C·克拉克的一句话：“任何足够先进的技术，都与魔法无异。”这千真万确。

展开英文原文

When I was young, I wasn’t sure what I was going to do when I got older. I thought the idea of inventing things would be cool, because I read a quote from Arthur C. Clarke: “A sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic.” That’s really true.

如果回到三百年前，你会因为今天我们习以为常的事物而被绑在火刑柱上烧死。能够飞行是疯狂的。能够远距离视物、通信，并在地球上几乎任何地方即时获取全世界的所有信息。这一切在过去都会被视作魔法。实际上，还不止如此。我们今天习以为常的许多事物，在过去，哪怕在魔法的领域里，也未曾被想象过。

展开英文原文

If you go back three hundred years, you’d be burned at the stake for things we take for granted today. Being able to fly is crazy. Being able to see over long distances, communicate, and instantly access all the world’s information from almost anywhere on the earth. This would all be considered magic in times past. It actually goes beyond that. Many things we take for granted today weren’t imagined in times past, even in the realm of magic.

我想，如果我能推进技术，那就像成为一名魔法师。那会非常酷。162

展开英文原文

I thought if I could advance technology, that would be like being a magician. That would be really cool.162

曾有一度，我考虑过以物理学为职业。但如今要真正推进物理学，你需要新的数据。物理学从根本上受制于工程的进步。

展开英文原文

At one point, I was thinking about doing physics as a career. But to really advance physics these days, you need new data. Physics is fundamentally governed by the progress of engineering.

总有人争论：“工程师和科学家，哪个更好？科学家不是更好吗？爱因斯坦不是最聪明的人吗？”就我个人而言，我认为工程更好，因为没有工程，你就没有任何新数据。你会触碰到极限。

展开英文原文

There is always debate about, “Which is better, engineers or scientists? Aren’t scientists better? Wasn’t Einstein the smartest person?” Personally, I think engineering is better because without engineering, you do not have any new data. You hit a limit.

你可以在现有数据限制的范围内表现得很聪明，但除非你有办法获得更多数据，否则就无法取得进展。伽利略制造了望远镜，这让他得以看到木星有卫星。如果你想推进文明，就必须解决那个限制因素。那个限制因素就是工程。因此，你必须解决工程问题。163

展开英文原文

You can be smart within the context of the limits of the data you have, but unless you have a way to get more data, you can’t make progress. Galileo engineered the telescope, which allowed him to see Jupiter had moons. If you want to advance civilization, you must address the limiting factor. The limiting factor is the engineering. Therefore, you must address the engineering.163

我当然钦佩伟大科学家的发现。他们正在更深入地理解宇宙既有的运作方式。这很酷——但从某种意义上说，宇宙本身早已知道这些。他们是在发现已经存在的事物。164

展开英文原文

I certainly admire the discoveries of the great scientists. They’re creating a deeper understanding of how the universe already works. That’s cool—but the universe, in a way, already knows that. They’re discovering what already exists.164

工程赢得战争

展开英文原文

ENGINEERING WINS WARS

在孙子的《孙子兵法》中，没有关于技术的章节。

展开英文原文

In Sun Tzu’s The Art of War, there is no chapter on technology.

这是一本有趣的书，我读过很多遍。书中充满智慧，但应该有一章说：“如果你拥有决定性的技术优势，就能以最小的伤亡赢得胜利。”166

展开英文原文

It is an interesting book I’ve read many times. It’s packed with wisdom, but there should be a chapter saying: “If you have a decisive technological advantage, you can win with minimal casualties to your side.”166

技术在战争中的作用远比人们通常理解的要大：从最广义的角度看技术，包括更好的方阵，或由青铜、铁、钢制成的长矛。这些都可能带来巨大差异。167

展开英文原文

Technology plays a much stronger role in war than is generally understood: technology viewed in the broadest sense, including a better phalanx, or spears made of bronze, iron, or steel. These can be big differences.167

罗马人通过技术赢得了战争。168

展开英文原文

The Romans won their wars through technology.168

罗马人的一个优势是拥有良好的冶金技术。他们的剑是马氏体的（相对于奥氏体冶金技术的改进），因此更坚固。罗马人经常与对手作战，对方的剑在罗马剑面前基本上会弯曲。如果你在剑斗中，你的剑像面条一样弯曲……那是巨大的劣势。169

展开英文原文

One of the advantages Romans had was good metallurgy. Their swords were martensitic (an improvement over austenitic metallurgy), so they were stronger. The Romans were often fighting opponents whose swords would basically bend over a Roman sword. If you’re in a sword fight and your sword bends like a noodle…big disadvantage.169

罗马人是伟大的工程师。即使是修路这样的事情也给他们带来了军事优势。如果你想快速行军，道路远比森林中蜿蜒的小径强得多。170

展开英文原文

The Romans were great engineers. Even things like building roads gave them a military advantage. If you’re trying to march an army somewhere fast, roads beat the heck out of a small winding path through the forest.170

但在罗马帝国境外作战时，他们有时会因为对手的技术而输掉战争。当罗马人与斯基泰人作战时，他们没有很好的对策来应对骑射弓箭手，尤其是被引诱到平坦地形时。面对骑射弓箭手的技术，他们几乎束手无策。171

展开英文原文

But when fighting outside the Roman empire, they sometimes lost their wars because of their opponent’s technology. When the Romans fought the Scythians, they did not have a good counter to mounted war archers, especially if they got lured into flat terrain. They were pretty much helpless against the technology of a mounted archer.171

当技术变革速度加快时，

工程就起着关键作用。

展开英文原文

When there’s a rapid change in the rate of

technology, engineering plays a pivotal role.

如果技术存在巨大差异——即使对方人数更多、将领更优秀、更聪明——拥有先进技术的一方也会获胜。172

展开英文原文

If there is a big difference in the technologies—even if the other side has more people, better generals, and is smarter— the side with the advanced technology will win.172

二战期间战斗机和轰炸机的技术战令人着迷。美国在战争末期在轰炸机方面完全碾压对手，但他们一开始并非如此。在太平洋战争初期，整个美国中队有时被击落，而日本零损失。彻底击倒。

展开英文原文

The technology war of fighters and bombers during World War II is fascinating. The US completely crushed it on bombers at the end of the war, but they didn’t start out that way. At the start of World War II in the Pacific, entire US squadrons were sometimes shot down with zero Japanese losses. A total KO.

二战初期的美国战斗机也不怎么样。他们的战术糟糕，飞机糟糕，训练也不正确。173

展开英文原文

The US fighters at the beginning of World War II were not good either. Their tactics were terrible, the aircraft were terrible, and the training was incorrect, too.173

看看他们起步时的状态以及创新速度之快，很有意思。许多国家都有令人印象深刻的设计工作：日本、美国、德国、英国、俄罗斯等国都有一些出色的战斗机设计。174

展开英文原文

It’s interesting to see where they started out and how fast things innovated. There was impressive design work by many countries: Japan, the US, Germany, UK, Russia, and others had some impressive fighter designs.174

这是一场持续的技术剪刀石头布游戏。一个国家制造一种飞机，另一个国家制造一种新飞机来击败它，然后另一个国家又制造一种更新的飞机。真正重要的是创新的速度。175

展开英文原文

It was a constant technological rock-paper-scissors game. One country would make a plane, another made a new plane to beat that one, then another country would make an even newer plane. What really matters is the pace of innovation.175

当技术变革的速度足够快，或者一方与另一方之间存在巨大的技术差异时，技术就会占据主导地位，你就会获得一边倒的胜利。176

展开英文原文

When the rate of change of technology is high enough, or there is a big technological difference between one side and the other, then that technology dominates and you get a lopsided victory.176

许多关于战争策略的书实际上并没有涉及技术，或者只是附带提及。但显然，如果拥有压倒性的技术优势，即使胜算极低，那一方也会获胜。177

展开英文原文

Many books on the strategy of war actually don’t address technology, or do only in a tangential manner. But obviously, if there is an overwhelming technological advantage, that side will win even if the odds are dramatically stacked against them.177

举一个极端的例子（极限情况），如果你能从太空发射激光，只需指向地面任何一点就能击中目标，那么无论你是在对抗尤利乌斯·凯撒、海因茨·古德里安还是拿破仑，都无关紧要。他们都会被太空激光击中。178

展开英文原文

To use an extreme example (a limit case), if you can shoot lasers from space to any spot on the ground by just pointing at it, it would not matter if you’re fighting Julius Caesar, Heinz Guderian, or Napoleon. They just got lasered from space.178

历史上大多数战役，由于技术发展缓慢，更多是关于机动、战术和战略。但当出现技术断层时，它会从根本上改变整个局势。现代战争在很大程度上是技术竞赛战争。我们能多快创造出新技术？最好的例子就是核弹。谁先造出核弹，谁就赢了。就是这样。故事结束。179

展开英文原文

Most battles in history, because technology moved slowly, were more about maneuvering, tactics, and strategy. But when there’s technological discontinuity, it fundamentally changes the whole situation. Wars in the modern era are very much technology race wars. How fast can we create new technology? The best example would be the nuclear bomb. Anyone who made nuclear bombs first, won. That’s it. End of story.179

这就是美国曼哈顿计划的原因。人们认为那是一个政府项目。我想强调的是，它更多是物理学界的创造，而非政府的。政府提供了支持，但这是物理学界的决定和创造。没有他们，这一切就不会发生。180

展开英文原文

That was the reason for the US Manhattan Project. People think it was a government project. I’d like to emphasize that it was a creation of the physics community more than it was the government. The government supported it, but it was a decision and creation from the physics community. Without them, it would not have happened.180

他们只是得出了一个结论：不能让希特勒拥有原子弹。所以他们先造了出来，以确保万无一失。

展开英文原文

They simply came to the conclusion that they couldn’t let Hitler have the bomb. So they made it first to be certain of it.

没有比这更好的超级武器例子了——谁得到它，谁就获胜。181

展开英文原文

There isn’t a better example of a super weapon—anyone who gets it wins.181

要么全力争胜，要么干脆别玩。182

展开英文原文

Play to win, or don’t play at all.182

Coils of aluminum and plastic pellets go into one end of the factory and cars come out the other. We did all the vehicle engineering, all the powertrain engineering, all the software. The evidence for us solving hard engineering problems is that Toyota, Daimler, and Mercedes buy electric powertrains from us. If it was easy, they would do it.185
