第一性原理思维

展开英文原文

FIRST-PRINCIPLES THINKING

问：第一性原理思维从何而来？

展开英文原文

Q: Where does first-principles thinking come from?

当你想做新的事情时，你必须运用物理学的方法。物理学家发现反直觉的新事物，比如量子力学。他们通过从“第一性原理”思考来做到这一点：从头开始构建推理。94

展开英文原文

When you want to do something new, you have to apply the physics approach. Physicists discover counterintuitive new things, like quantum mechanics. They do that by thinking from “first principles”: building their reasoning from the ground up.94

我鼓励人们使用物理学的思维工具，并将其广泛应用于生活中。它们是最好的工具。95

展开英文原文

I would encourage people to use the mental tools

of physics and apply them broadly in life. They are

the best tools.95

我们通常的生活方式是类比推理。

展开英文原文

The normal way we conduct our lives is reasoning by analogy.

这意味着我们做某事，是因为它与别的事相似，或者别人也在这么做。

展开英文原文

That means we do something because it’s similar to something else, or what other people are doing.

这样思考时，你只能得到微小的迭代。类比推理比从第一性原理出发更容易，所以我们大多数时候都这么做。96 而且在生活中的大多数情况下，我们确实应该用类比推理。否则，精神上你根本无法度过一天。那会需要太多思考。97

展开英文原文

When you think this way, you only get slight iterations. It’s easier to reason by analogy rather than from first principles, so that’s what we do most of the time.96 And in most of life, we should reason by analogy. Otherwise, mentally, you wouldn’t be able to get through the day. It would be too much thinking.97

但对于重要的事情，这种思维方式过于受惯例或先前经验的束缚。你会听到“一直都是这么做的”或“从来没人这样做过”。这是一种荒谬的思考方式。

展开英文原文

But for important things, that kind of thinking is too bound by convention or prior experiences. You will hear, “It’s always been done this way,” or “Nobody’s ever done it.” That is a ridiculous way to think.

不要只是追随潮流。你可以通过用物理学的方法，即第一性原理来思考，从而避免追随潮流。这对一般生活来说，是一种非常非常强大的方法。

展开英文原文

Don’t just follow the trends. You can avoid following trends by thinking with the physics approach, first principles. It’s a powerful, powerful method for life in general.

审视基本原理，并由此构建你的推理。然后看看你得出的结论是否可行。98 它可能与人们过去所做的不同，也可能相同。99

展开英文原文

Look at the fundamentals and construct your reasoning from there. Then see if you have a conclusion that works or doesn’t work.98 It might or might not be different from what people have done in the past.99

问：你如何应用第一性原理思维？

展开英文原文

Q: How do you apply first-principles thinking?

将事物分解到最根本的原理。首先问自己：在基础层面上，我最确信什么是真实的？这设定了你的公理基础。然后你由此向上推理。接着，你将结论与这些公理真理进行核对。

展开英文原文

Break something down to the most fundamental principles. Start by asking: What am I most confident is true at a foundational level? That sets your axiomatic base. Then you reason up from there. Then you check your conclusions against the axiomatic truths.

例如，处理任何新技术问题时，要通过第一性原理分析确保你没有违反物理学。物理学中的一个基本问题是：我是否违反了能量守恒或动量守恒？如果是，那它就行不通。这只是为了确定这个想法是否可能。100

展开英文原文

For instance, to approach any new technology problem, make sure you’re not violating physics with a first-principles analysis. A basic question in physics would be: Am I violating conservation of energy or momentum? If so, it’s not going to work. That’s just to establish if this idea is possible.100

这样思考很难。需要付出大量努力。但如果你想尝试新事物，这是最好的思考方式。101

展开英文原文

It’s hard to think this way. It takes a lot of effort. But if you’re trying to do something new, it’s the best way to think.101

问：你是如何将第一性原理思维应用于创建公司的？

展开英文原文

Q: How have you applied first-principles thinking to building companies?

这里有一个特斯拉创建初期的例子。人们说电池组太贵，无法制造便宜的电动汽车。他们假设电池会一直昂贵，因为过去一直如此。这相当愚蠢。如果你把这种推理应用于一切新事物，那么你永远不会尝试任何新东西。“哦，没人想要汽车。马很棒；我们习惯了它们。它们能吃草。到处都有很多草。没有汽油可用。所以人们永远不会买汽油车。”人们确实这么说过，而且说了很多。

展开英文原文

Here’s an example from early in building Tesla. People said battery packs were too expensive to make cheap electric cars. They assumed they would always be expensive, because they had been in the past. That’s pretty dumb. If you applied that reasoning to everything new, then you would never try anything new. “Oh, nobody wants a car. Horses are great; we’re used to them. They can eat grass. There’s lots of grass all over the place. There’s no gasoline available. So people will never buy gas cars.” People did say that, a lot.

人们假设电动汽车的电池每千瓦时成本永远是600美元。对电池成本的第一性原理方法是：电池是由什么制成的？构成电池的材料是什么？这些材料成分的市场价值是多少？

展开英文原文

People assumed batteries for electric vehicles would always cost $600 per kilowatt hour. The first-principles approach to battery costs is this: What are the batteries made of? What are the materials that make up the batteries? What is the market value of those material constituents?

它含有钴、镍、铝、碳、一些用于分离的聚合物，以及一个钢壳。好，如果我们在伦敦金属交易所购买这些数量的材料，每样东西会花多少钱？哦，天哪，每千瓦时只要80美元。所以很明显，我们只需要想出巧妙的方法，把这些材料组合成电池芯的形状。我就是这样知道，有可能制造出比任何人意识到的都便宜得多的电池。102

展开英文原文

It’s got cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, some polymers for separation, and a steel can. Okay, what if we bought that amount of material at the London Metal Exchange? What would each of those things cost? Oh, geez, it’s only $80 per kilowatt hour. So clearly, we just need to think of clever ways to take those materials and combine them into the shape of a battery cell. That’s how I knew it was possible to build batteries much much cheaper than anyone else realized.102

世界上存在着巨大的技术差异，

即使是硬核技术专家也未曾察觉。

展开英文原文

Great differences in technology exist in the world,

展开英文原文

第一性原理方法是理解新事物可能性的好途径。这并不意味着你一定会成功，但至少可以判断成功是否属于可能性之一，这一点很重要。我就是这样决定创办SpaceX的。104

展开英文原文

The first-principles approach is a good way to understand what new things are possible. It doesn’t mean you’ll be successful, but at least you can determine if success is one of the possibilities, and that is important. This is how I decided to start SpaceX.104

第一性原理思维造就了SpaceX。大多数人会想：“历史上，所有火箭都很昂贵。因此，未来所有火箭也会很昂贵。”但事实并非如此。105

展开英文原文

First-principles thinking built SpaceX. Most people think, “Historically, all rockets have been expensive. Therefore, in the future, all rockets will be expensive.” But that’s not true.105

这时再次运用分析方法就很有帮助。106

展开英文原文

This is where it’s helpful to use the analytical approach again.106

我们将第一性原理思维应用于火箭制造的方式是问：“制造火箭的材料是什么？”107火箭由铝、钛、铜和碳纤维制成。进一步分解，再问：“每种材料用了多少？那么，所有这些原材料的成本是多少？”108

展开英文原文

The way we applied first-principles thinking to rocketry was asking, “What are the materials that go into a rocket?”107 A rocket is made from aluminum, titanium, copper, and carbon fiber. Break it down further and ask, “How much of each material is used? Now, what is the cost of all these raw components?”108

如果把这些材料堆在地上，挥动魔杖就能造出火箭，那么火箭的成本会是多少？我们设想重新排列原子的成本为零。109

展开英文原文

If you have them stacked on the floor and could wave a magic wand to create the rocket, what would the cost of the rocket be? We imagine the cost of rearranging the atoms was zero.109

这将设定火箭成本的下限。我称之为“魔杖数字”，即假设的最佳情况。对于火箭来说，这个数字相对较小，远低于当前成本的5%，有时接近1%或2%。110如果原材料成本仅占成品的1%或2%，那么制造过程一定非常低效。111

展开英文原文

That’s going to set the floor of the cost of the rocket. I call this the “magic wand number,” the hypothetical best-case scenario. For rockets, that turned out to be a relatively small number, well under 5 percent of the current cost, in some cases closer to 1 or 2 percent.110 The manufacturing must be very inefficient if the raw material cost is only 1 or 2 percent of the finished product.111

我看到了巨大的改进空间。112现在我们的挑战是找出如何更高效地将原子塑造成正确形状。113

展开英文原文

I was able to see a great deal of room for improvement.112 Now our challenge was to figure out how to get the atoms in the right shape more efficiently.113

这种围绕火箭的第一性原理思维过程，后来适用于所有部件。我称之为“白痴指数”。

展开英文原文

That first-principles thought process around the rocket became general purpose for all parts. I call it “The Idiot Index.”

展开英文原文

展开英文原文

火箭的一个部件，半喷嘴套，成本为13000美元。但它仅由价值200美元的钢材制成。我希望我所有的工程师随时都能根据白痴指数，了解他们系统中最好和最差的部件。115

展开英文原文

One part of the rocket, the half nozzle jacket, cost $13,000. But it was only made of $200 worth of steel. I expect all my engineers to know all the best and worst parts in their systems as judged by the idiot index at all times.115

这就是我所说的从第一性原理角度思考问题。如果我通过类比来分析，说：“其他所有火箭公司在做什么？他们的火箭成本是多少？历史上其他火箭的成本是多少？”那是类比推理，但它并不能真正说明真正的潜力所在。116

展开英文原文

That’s what I mean by thinking about things from a firstprinciples standpoint. If I had analyzed it by analogy and said, “What are all other rocket companies doing? What do their rockets cost? What historically have other rockets cost?” That is reasoning by analogy, but it really doesn’t illustrate what the true potential is.116

极限思维

展开英文原文

THINKING IN LIMITS

另一个有用的物理工具是“在极限中”思考问题。选取一个特定的想法，想象将其放大到极大或缩小到极小。事物会如何变化？118

展开英文原文

Another good physics tool is thinking about things “in the limit.” Take a particular idea and imagine scaling it to a very large or very small number. How do things change?118

无聊公司就是一个很好的例子。对隧道构想的一个常见批评是：隧道会被用尽，交通拥堵仍将存在。他们没有意识到，隧道层数实际上没有真正的限制。你可以向地下深处延伸，远比向上建造要远得多。最深的矿井比最高的建筑要深得多。

展开英文原文

The Boring Company is a great example. A common criticism of the idea of tunnels is: The tunnel will get used up and there will still be traffic congestion. They don’t realize there’s no real limit to how many levels of tunnel you can have. You can go much farther deep underground than you can build up. The deepest mines are much deeper than the tallest buildings are tall.

你是否注意到，城市是三维建造的，但道路却只建在二维平面上？你可以通过在城市下方挖掘隧道来建造三维道路。119 借助三维隧道网络，任何程度的城市拥堵都能得到缓解。120

展开英文原文

Ever notice that cities are built in 3D, but roads are only built in 2D? You could build roads in 3D by building tunnels under cities.119 You can alleviate any amount of urban congestion with a 3D tunnel network.120

然而，以目前的方式挖掘隧道既困难又昂贵。洛杉矶地铁延伸段每英里的建造成本约为十亿美元。121

展开英文原文

But, it’s difficult and expensive to dig tunnels the way they’re currently done. The LA subway extension cost roughly a billion dollars per mile to build.121

只需做到两点，就能让隧道掘进

获得约一个数量级的提升。

展开英文原文

If you do just two things, you can get

approximately an order of magnitude

improvement in tunneling.

但我认为甚至可以超越这个水平。122

展开英文原文

But I think you can even go beyond that.122

我们需要将每英里隧道的成本至少降低十倍。首先要做的是缩小隧道直径。根据现行法规，单车道隧道的直径必须在二十六到二十八英尺之间。但如果将直径缩小到十二英尺，截面积就会减少到原来的四分之一。这是一个巨大的改进，因为隧道成本与截面积成正比。仅此一项，每英里成本就能获得近半个数量级（4到5倍）的提升。123

展开英文原文

We need at least a tenfold improvement in the cost per mile of tunneling. The first thing to do is to cut the tunnel diameter. According to current regulations, a single road-lane tunnel has to be twenty-six to twenty-eight feet in diameter. But if you shrink that diameter to twelve feet, the area goes down by a factor of four. This is a huge improvement, because the cost of tunneling scales with the area. That’s almost a half-order of magnitude (4–5x) improvement in cost per mile right there.123

目前，隧道掘进机一半时间在工作，另一半时间则停下来进行隧道壁加固。如果设计出能连续掘进并同时加固的机器，就能将效率提高一倍。两者结合，就是八倍的提升。124

展开英文原文

Currently, tunneling machines work for half the time, then stop. The other half of the time is putting in reinforcements for the tunnel wall. If you design the machine instead to do continuous tunneling and reinforcing, that will give you a factor of two improvement. Combine those and that’s a factor of eight.124

这些机器还远未达到其功率或热极限，因此我们应该大幅提高机器功率。在这方面至少还能再获得两倍的提升，甚至可能达到四到五倍。目前，我们离隧道技术的极限还差得很远。125

展开英文原文

These machines are also far from being at their power or thermal limits, so we should jack up the power to the machine. We can get at least another factor of two there, maybe even four or five. Currently, we’re not even close to the limits of tunneling technology.125

问：极限思维如何应用于制造业？

展开英文原文

Q: How does thinking in limits apply to manufacturing?

假设我们试图弄清楚某个零件或产品为何昂贵。是因为某些愚蠢的原因，还是仅仅因为产量太低？126

展开英文原文

Let’s say we’re trying to figure out why a part or product is expensive. Is it because of something foolish, or just because production volume is too low?126
